A reusable floral recipe template

A floral recipe should let another person rebuild the design without guessing what was in the original photo. Pair a material list with the small production details that a photo cannot show.

By Gilad Ronen · Updated October 23, 2026

What belongs on the card?

  • Design name and collection: use a name you can find again, such as “Low garden centerpiece — spring.”
  • Vessel: record the container and practical preparation notes.
  • Material and color or variety: use consistent names so matching materials can combine on a pull sheet.
  • Quantity per arrangement: record the amount used for one finished design, separate from an allowance.
  • Waste allowance: record an explicit percentage for each material if you need one.
  • Reference photo and notes: capture proportions, substitutions, assembly order, and anything easy to misread.

Check the handoff before copying the recipe

Design identity
Give the sample a name and revision. If the bowl changes, check the actual recipe before reusing it.
Units
Write “stems” beside rose quantities. Keep ribbon lengths, vessels, and supplier bunch counts in their own units.
Substitutions
Record an approved alternative or write “confirm before substituting.” Similar color does not establish the same size or stem count.

Make it reusable

  1. Build the sample and compare the recorded quantities with what was actually used.
  2. Choose one material naming convention. “White rose” and “Rose / White” are easy for a person to understand but are different records.
  3. Keep the recipe quantities for one arrangement. Set the batch quantity when planning the run.
  4. Review the notes whenever the vessel, material grade, or design changes.
