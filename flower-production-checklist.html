A flower production checklist

A recipe records the design. A production plan records what the team is making today. Keep those jobs separate so a late quantity change does not rewrite your master recipe.

By Gilad Ronen · Updated October 20, 2026

1. Confirm the design before scaling

Start with the approved sample, vessel, recipe, and reference photo. Check whether substitutions change the quantity per arrangement. A photo alone may hide the material count or the way the design is assembled.

If a recipe has changed, update it before making the run. Avoid writing one version on a station card and using another version for the pull list.

2. Make a batch plan

List every design and the number of finished arrangements required. In a simple example, a run might contain 12 low centerpieces and 5 bouquets. Keep spare arrangements separate from material allowances: one is an extra finished design; the other is extra material.

3. Calculate and review the pull

Scale each material line, apply its recorded allowance, and round up. Combine matching material names and colors only after calculating the recipe lines. Review totals against usable materials and pack sizes separately. See the worked calculation for the exact approach PetalPull uses.

4. Prepare the station handoff

  • Give each station the correct recipe, vessel, finished quantity, and reference photo.
  • Include instructions that cannot be inferred from the picture, such as the finished size or an approved substitution.
  • Print a clear card and verify the first copy at the intended paper size.
  • Make sure a quantity update reaches both the material pull and the station instructions.

PetalPull can print a 5×7 recipe card or share a PDF through iOS. The card explains the repeat design; the consolidated pull sheet brings together the run's matching materials.

5. Close the loop

After the run, record what changed: substitutions, a quantity correction, or a clearer assembly instruction. Save useful changes back into the reusable recipe after checking them. Make a fresh backup of a library that has gained important new work.
