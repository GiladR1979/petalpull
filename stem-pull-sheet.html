Calculate a floral stem pull sheet

A pull sheet turns a recipe for one arrangement into the materials needed for a production run. Keep the quantity used in the design separate from any extra allowance.

By Gilad Ronen · Updated October 20, 2026

The calculation

For one material line, multiply the quantity per arrangement by the number of arrangements, then multiply by one plus the allowance percentage. Round up to a whole unit.

Pull quantity = round up(quantity × arrangements × (1 + allowance ÷ 100))

For example, 6 white roses × 12 centerpieces × 1.10 = 79.2. The pull is 80 white roses. The 10% allowance here is an example, not a universal recommendation.

Combine recipes after calculating their lines

Suppose you also need 5 bouquets, each with 8 white roses and the same example 10% allowance. Their pull is 8 × 5 × 1.10 = 44. Add that to the centerpiece pull: 80 + 44 = 124 white roses.

PetalPull rounds up each recipe material line first, then combines matching names and colors. That can differ from adding all unrounded amounts and rounding only once. For example, two separate lines of 1 stem with 10% allowance each round to 2; together they pull 4, not 3.

Keep matching names consistent

PetalPull groups materials by name and color or variety, ignoring outer spaces and capitalization. “Rose / White” and “rose / white” combine. “Rose / Ivory” stays separate from “Rose / White.” A spelling difference, abbreviation, or plural can keep lines apart.

Pull quantity is not a supplier order

A calculated pull is the requirement for the run. Compare it with usable stock and your supplier's pack sizes before ordering. This page and PetalPull do not check live stock, sell flowers, or submit supplier orders.

Before handing off the list

  • Confirm the approved recipe and number of finished arrangements.
  • Check the unit: stems, bunches, and packs are not interchangeable.
  • Review any allowance for the actual material and conditions.
  • Keep colors or varieties separate when they cannot substitute.
  • Recalculate if the production quantity changes.
